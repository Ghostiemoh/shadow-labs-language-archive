## Design Context

### Users
- **First priority: contributors on phones.** Native Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo speakers recording their voice, correcting AI drafts and adding names, mostly on mid-range Android phones, often outdoors or on slow, expensive data. They need big tap targets, fast pages, clear next steps, and text that renders tone marks and special letters (ẹ ọ ṣ ɓ ɗ ƙ ụ ị ṅ) perfectly.
- **Public visitors** arriving from X and Instagram posts to look up a word, see the picture and hear it said.
- **Funders and data buyers** (grant reviewers, AI companies, researchers) judging credibility at a glance: is this careful, licensed, reviewed work?
- When needs conflict, the contributor on a phone wins.

### Brand Personality
- **Proud, warm, scholarly.**
- A serious archive of Nigerian languages built with care, like a well-kept museum catalogue, with the warmth and colour of a market day. Confident about the languages, never apologetic or exoticising.
- Emotional goals: pride for speakers ("my language, done properly"), trust for funders, curiosity for visitors.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Light theme: readable in daylight on phones. **Clay Archive** palette (see `brand.md`): a clay-red field (#82221E) with cream type for the hero, warm paper elsewhere, ochre square data markers, warm ink.
- Design reference chosen by the founder (26 Nov 2026): **AI Philosophy** on motionsites.ai — heritage image in halftone on a red field, large grotesk headlines ("Words that last. / Voices that endure.").
- The words themselves are the hero: large, beautifully set names with correct diacritics, photos second, chrome last.
- Cultural references come from real materials and objects (indigo cloth, clay, woven mats, calabash, strip-woven cloth), used sparingly and structurally, not as wallpaper.
- **Anti-references:**
  - Generic SaaS or startup look: purple gradients, glassy cards, AI-template layouts.
  - Tourist "African print" cliché: kente or ankara patterns as decoration, safari imagery.
  - Dusty academic site: grey, wall-of-text university pages.
  - Childish learning app: mascots, gamified badges, bubbly fonts.

### Accessibility and constraints
- WCAG AA contrast minimum; body text never below 16px on mobile.
- Fonts must fully support Yoruba and Igbo combining diacritics and Hausa hooked letters; never clip stacked marks (generous line-height on words).
- Low bandwidth: small images (WebP), few font files, little JavaScript on public pages.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Design Principles
1. **The word comes first.** Every screen should make the name in each language the most legible, most beautiful thing on it.
2. **Phone-first, data-light.** Design for a thumb, sunlight and a slow connection before a desktop.
3. **Show the care.** Review status, credits, licenses and dialect are visible and dignified, not hidden in footnotes; they are the proof of quality.
4. **Rooted, not decorated.** Draw on Nigerian materials and objects for structure and colour, with restraint; no pattern wallpaper or clichés.
5. **One clear next step.** Contributors always know what to do next: record, correct, submit, next task.
